Talking to the terminal

Security work often happens in the terminal. The language of terminal commands is called bash. Most people use a computer by clicking buttons and windows. Bash lets you do more, like change who can open a file.

Each challenge below needs one command, on one line. Type the full command, the same way you would type it in the terminal. This page does not run your command. It checks that the command is written correctly.

Review: key ideas

Commands. A command starts with its name. After the name, you add the details it needs, like file names. Spaces separate the parts.

ls -l day.txt

Here, ls is the command name. -l is an option: it changes how the command works. day.txt is a file name. File names are case-sensitive, so day.txt and Day.txt are two different files.

echo prints text on the screen.

echo "Good morning"
# prints on the screen: Good morning

Quote marks around the text are a good habit. If your text has an apostrophe, like I'm, use double quotes: "I'm here".

Permissions. Every file has permissions. They decide who can read the file (open it), write to it (change it), and execute it (run it as a program). The command ls -l shows the permissions as letters, at the start of the line:

-rw-r--r--  1 ana  staff  24 Sep 23 10:00 day.txt

Skip the first character. The next nine letters are three groups of three: one for the owner of the file, one for the file’s group of users, and one for everyone else. In each group, r means read, w means write, x means execute, and - means “not allowed.”

-   rw-     r--     r--
    owner   group   everyone else

Here, the owner can read and write. The group and everyone else can only read.

Your research. To solve the challenges, you also need to learn three things: how to send text into a file, how to write permissions with numbers, and how to use cp. Search online to find them. This is part of the practice. If you need help, use the hints.

Thinking like a blue team member

In this part, you think like a blue team member. Read the story below. It is about ransomware: harmful software that locks a company’s files until the company pays money. Then answer three questions about the story.

There is no single right answer. This part is about thinking and research, so search online to learn more. Each answer needs at least 100 words. The page checks only the length, not what you write. It saves your writing in this browser, so you can return to it later.

The storyHappy Hippo Co. is a small company that makes toys. One sunny afternoon, the employees returned to their desks. They could not open any of their files. A message was on their screens. The attacker wanted a payment of 50 bitcoins. After the payment, the attacker would give them the decryption key, which unlocks the company’s important data. The message said:

“Your files have been encrypted and are no longer accessible. Please follow the instructions below to obtain the decryption key. Payment must be made within 72 hours, or the decryption key will be permanently deleted.”

This means: the company cannot open its files. It must pay within 72 hours to get the key. If it does not pay, the key will be deleted forever.

The IT team quickly understood what happened. Ransomware had attacked their server and encrypted the company’s important data. The team tried to isolate the server, but it was too late. The ransomware had already spread to all the other computers on the network.

The CEO (the head of the company) called a friend, Bob the White Hat Hacker. Bob is a cybersecurity expert. He studied the attack and wrote a report. The report said that the attack started when an employee clicked on a phishing email. This let the attacker enter the company’s network without permission. Bob also said that the attacker used very strong encryption, called RSA-2048. He told the company not to pay the attackers. He said they should restore their data from their backups instead.

The company made backups of its data regularly. So the IT team restored the data quickly, and Happy Hippo Co. was soon working again.
Review: words in the story
  • Encrypt: to change data into a secret code, so no one can read it without a key. The decryption key makes the data readable again. RSA-2048 is a very strong kind of encryption. Without the key, it is almost impossible to unlock the files.
  • Bitcoin: a kind of digital money. Attackers often ask for it because it is hard to know who received the payment.
  • Phishing email: a fake email that tries to trick a person. It may ask them to click a link, open a file, or type a password.
  • Server: a computer that stores files or runs programs for other computers. A network is a group of computers that are connected, so they can share files and data.
  • IT team: the people who manage a company’s computers and network.
  • Isolate: to disconnect a computer from the network, so an attack cannot spread to other computers.
  • Backup: an extra copy of data, kept in a safe place. To restore data means to use the backup to replace data that was lost or locked.
  • White hat hacker: a security expert who uses hacking skills to protect people, not to harm them.
